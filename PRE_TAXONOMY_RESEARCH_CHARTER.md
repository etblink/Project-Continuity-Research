# Pre-Taxonomy Research Charter

Version: 0.1.0
Frozen: 2026-09-20
Status: Research framing only; not an architecture specification.

## 1. Research Objective

Discover the minimum durable architecture required for successive humans and
AI agents to preserve truthful understanding, coherent purpose, human agency,
and effective direction across arbitrarily long projects.

This research should not assume in advance that a "Project Kernel" is the
final architecture. Project Kernel is currently a candidate component within a
broader continuity system.

## 2. Primary Empirical Question

Which executive and metacognitive functions has the human operator repeatedly
had to supply during long-running AI-assisted work, and how might those
functions be externalized into durable, portable architecture?

## 3. Candidate First Principle: Logos

### Working interpretation

Logos is treated here as a candidate principle of fidelity to reality:
reason, intelligibility, coherence, truth, evidence, proper distinctions,
contradiction detection, and an ordered relationship between claims and the
evidence that supports them.

### Logos hypothesis

Long-horizon AI systems require mechanisms that continually restore alignment
between representation and reality.

### Questions associated with Logos

- What is actually true?
- What evidence establishes it?
- Which source or artifact is authoritative?
- Is a claim current, frozen, historical, or superseded?
- Are intention and demonstrated state being confused?
- Does the conclusion actually follow from the evidence?
- What remains uncertain?
- Have the reasons behind prior decisions been preserved?

## 4. Candidate First Principle: Agape

### Working interpretation

Agape is treated here as a candidate design principle of orientation toward the
good of the other: intelligence exercised in service rather than domination,
manipulation, vanity, or merely satisfying the nearest local objective.

This does not imply that an AI experiences love, care, or moral emotion.
The term is being used as a normative architectural lens.

### Agape hypothesis

Long-horizon AI systems require mechanisms that continually restore alignment
between execution and the human or community good the project exists to serve.

### Questions associated with Agape

- What or whom is this project ultimately for?
- Is the system preserving human agency?
- Is the local optimization damaging the larger purpose?
- Are long-term consequences being considered?
- Is uncertainty being concealed because confidence is more satisfying?
- Is the system serving the project, or merely completing the task?
- Can people other than the original creator understand and use the system?

## 5. Relationship Between the Two

A working proposition:

> Truth without care can optimize the wrong thing.
> Care without truth can protect an illusion.

The research should test whether this pairing is useful, incomplete, or
misleading.

Neither Logos nor Agape is currently a frozen architectural requirement.

## 6. Empirical Discipline

The failure taxonomy must be derived from observed incidents rather than forced
into preselected philosophical categories.

For every historical incident, ask:

### What happened?

- What did the AI believe?
- What was actually true?
- What objective was it locally pursuing?
- What larger purpose should have governed it?
- What did the human notice?
- What intervention corrected the trajectory?
- Why did that intervention work?
- Could the system have detected the problem itself?

### Failure dimensions

- Epistemic / Logos failure?
- Purpose / Agape failure?
- Memory failure?
- Authority failure?
- Orientation failure?
- Planning failure?
- Succession failure?
- Other?

The `Other?` category is mandatory. Evidence must be allowed to reveal
dimensions not anticipated by the current framing.

### Missing function

What executive or metacognitive function did the human have to supply?

### Architectural implication

What mechanism might externalize that function?

## 7. Separation of Philosophy and Taxonomy

The design philosophy asks:

> What qualities should a successful system preserve?

The empirical taxonomy asks:

> What repeatedly went wrong in actual long-horizon human-AI work?

The two may inform one another, but neither should be allowed to manufacture
the other.

## 8. Public-Generalization Requirement

This research is intended to produce something usable beyond the originating
projects.

Therefore the eventual architecture should aim to be:

- model-independent;
- vendor-neutral;
- human-readable;
- Git-compatible but not Git-dependent;
- usable by people who did not invent it;
- scalable from solo human-AI work to multi-project and multi-agent systems.

Project Observatory and its subordinate projects are the initial laboratory,
not the final product.

## 9. Next Major Intellectual Operation

Mine the user's actual project history for concrete incidents of drift,
succession failure, authority confusion, stale-state persistence, local
optimization, loss of negative knowledge, purpose failure, evidence failure,
and other recurring breakdowns.

Do not design the final architecture yet.

The first output should be an empirical incident corpus and a provisional
failure taxonomy grounded in those incidents.
