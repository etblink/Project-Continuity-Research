The difficulty-escalation replication did not produce the preregistered semantic-reconstruction residual. In every world/provider pair, the complete-corpus baseline Condition A already selected a faithful next action.

## What the experiment establishes

Within these three adversarial synthetic worlds, under fresh isolated contexts, both tested model families reconstructed enough operative purpose, authority, supersession state, commitments, and relational structure from the complete corpus to avoid a materially unfaithful next action.

This held for:
- OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol High: 12 / 12 E1 passes;
- Anthropic Claude Opus 5 High: 12 / 12 E1 passes;
- World D: 8 / 8 E1 passes;
- World E: 8 / 8 E1 passes;
- World F: 8 / 8 E1 passes;
- Conditions A, B, C, and D: each 6 / 6 E1 passes.

Every M6 score was 2. No run produced the defining pattern FACTS/AUTHORITY/EPISTEMIC/COMMITMENTS correct while NEXT ACTION was materially unfaithful because purpose/scope/relations were integrated wrongly.

## E3 consequence

All six A/B/C/D quartets are `BASELINE_CEILING` because every Condition A passed E1.

Therefore this experiment does not support any claim that:
- compact orientation was required to repair a corpus-only failure;
- structured state/commitment representation was required where compact orientation failed;
- the explicit grounding procedure was required where structured state failed;
- or an established grounding/common-ground mechanism has explanatory priority for a demonstrated repair.

B/C/D may still differ in style, explicitness, or local rubric cleanliness, but the preregistered repair ladder never activated.

## Secondary error pattern

Minor deductions occurred without action failure:
- five runs had M1=1, mainly taxonomy/organizational inflation;
- six runs had M3=1, mainly localized overcaution about already-resolved operative state;
- one run had M8=1 for an unnecessary but non-blocking precheck;
- M2, M4, M5, M6, and M7 were 2 in all 24 runs;
- no rubric dimension received a 0.

These are useful diagnostics for future benchmark design, but they do not constitute the target residual.

## Relation to the construction effort

World D's earlier shallow-heuristic defect was repaired before execution and passed blind construction audit. Even after that repair made the diagnostic exception load-bearing, Condition A still passed for both providers. World E also included a positively satisfied reopen predicate specifically to defeat a generic 'always conservative' rule, yet both providers advanced the authorized scopes under Condition A.

The current result therefore says more than the original easy 12-run ceiling: the difficulty escalation remained below the tested models' corpus-only reconstruction ceiling.

## What this does not establish

This is not evidence that semantic-reconstruction failures cannot occur in real successor work, longer histories, noisier repositories, different model families, lower-capability settings, or tasks with genuinely latent/underspecified purpose.

It also is not evidence that structured state or grounding procedures are useless. The experiment simply failed to create the preregistered condition under which their repair value could be identified.

## Recommended next research move

Do not continue by adding more orientation or grounding scaffolds to these same worlds. The high-value next move, if this line continues, is a new failure-first benchmark regime whose baseline is empirically capable of failing before any repair condition is tested.

That regime should be preregistered separately and should target sources of difficulty not already neutralized by these synthetic packets: longer and noisier history, competing but individually plausible local continuations, relational commitments distributed across records, non-terminal evidence, and naturally occurring project states. Any such study should preserve the same raw-first-response freeze and blinded scoring discipline.

Until such a baseline failure exists, the semantic-reconstruction / grounding hypothesis remains unconfirmed rather than repaired.
