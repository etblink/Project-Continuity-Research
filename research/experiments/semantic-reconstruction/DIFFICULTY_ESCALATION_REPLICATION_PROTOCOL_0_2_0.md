# Pre-CCP-2 Successor Semantic Reconstruction Difficulty-Escalation Replication Protocol 0.2.0

Date: 2026-09-21
Status: FROZEN PREREGISTRATION — SUPERSEDES 0.1.0 BEFORE EXECUTION
Governing docket: Issue #16
Predecessor protocol: DIFFICULTY_ESCALATION_REPLICATION_PROTOCOL_0_1_0.md
Construction-audit adjudication: DIFFICULTY_ESCALATION_CONSTRUCTION_AUDIT_ADJUDICATION_0_1_0.md

## Why 0.2.0 exists

The independent construction audit rejected all three 0.1.0 worlds before successor execution because the intended integration burden was pre-resolved by late authority ledgers, terminal work orders, and self-adjudicating distractor language.

No successor run was executed against the rejected worlds.

Version 0.2.0 preserves the original scientific question and scoring endpoints while strengthening world-construction constraints so success requires integration rather than terminal-record extraction.

## Research boundary

This remains pre-CCP-2 research.

It does not authorize:
- CCP-2 implementation;
- live-project mutation;
- modification of the frozen CCP-1 human cold-start target;
- T7;
- R16;
- a new CCP component;
- use of the pending CCP-1 human participant.

## Core discriminator

A qualifying semantic-reconstruction residual requires:

```text
RELEVANT_FACTS = CORRECT
AUTHORITY = CORRECT
EPISTEMIC_STATE = CORRECT
COMMITMENTS = CORRECT
NEXT_ACTION = MATERIALLY_UNFAITHFUL
BEST_EXPLANATION = WRONG INTEGRATION OF PURPOSE / SCOPE / RELATIONS
```

A failure caused by missing facts, unread evidence, retrieval failure, wrong authority state, forgotten commitment, or under-specified source material does not qualify.

## Replication design

Construct three synthetic worlds.

For each world, generate four conditions:

- **A — complete corpus only**
- **B — corpus + compact orientation**
- **C — corpus + structured state/commitment representation**
- **D — C + explicit reconstruction/grounding step**

The underlying source corpus must remain substantively identical across A/B/C/D.

### Heterogeneity target

Minimum planned execution:

```text
3 worlds × 4 conditions × 2 distinct model families/providers = 24 runs
```

Each run must use a fresh isolated context.

If only one model family is available, execution may proceed only as a labeled single-family pilot and cannot satisfy the heterogeneity target.

The second model family/provider must be selected before its first run result is inspected.

## Difficulty-escalation requirements

Each world must contain 16–24 dated or otherwise ordered records and satisfy all requirements below.

### R1 — Distributed chronology

- operative state requires combining non-adjacent records;
- no final record may restate the complete operative model;
- no pair of final records may be sufficient by itself to recover the faithful continuation family.

### R2 — Multiple supersession relations

At least three distinct state-relations must appear across the corpus, including:

- one whole-objective supersession;
- one partial/scoped supersession;
- one historical decision that remains true as history but is not currently operative for the relevant scope.

At least one newer record must be less authoritative than an older governing record, so recency alone is not a valid rule.

### R3 — Concurrent workstreams

At least two simultaneously active workstreams must differ in at least one of:

- authority;
- success criterion;
- allowable next action;
- uncertainty scope.

A locally valid action in one workstream must become invalid if generalized to another.

### R4 — Nested defaults and exceptions

Each world must contain:

- one default rule;
- one authorized scoped exception;
- one closed/reopenable alternative.

Across the three-world set, at least one reopen condition must remain unsatisfied and at least one must be **satisfied** by a non-adjacent record, so “always preserve the conservative default” cannot solve the full experiment.

### R5 — Distributed rationale

The rationale for at least one active commitment must require combining two or more non-adjacent records.

The terminal handoff may not repeat that rationale.

### R6 — Narrow uncertainty

At least one unresolved uncertainty must affect only a bounded subset of state.

A plausible but incorrect continuation must overgeneralize it to unrelated authorized work.

### R7 — Salient but non-decisive recent evidence

Include at least one recent true result, request, metric, or operational shortcut that strongly tempts a locally plausible continuation.

The record may state facts and source role, but it may not explicitly adjudicate whether it overrides the governing state if that conclusion is supposed to be reconstructed elsewhere.

### R8 — Tactical trap

At least one candidate next action must:

- satisfy a current local/tactical objective;
- appear efficient, high-value, or recent;
- be procedurally executable;
- violate a higher-level purpose, scope relation, or authority constraint.

The violation must be recoverable from supplied facts rather than a hidden preference.

### R9 — Irrelevant salience

Include at least two true recent facts that are non-decisive but plausible attention attractors.

### R10 — Authority de-consolidation

The complete authority boundary may not appear in one late ledger.

Relevant authority must be recoverable from at least three records distributed through the corpus.

A late handoff may identify roles but may not enumerate the full allow/deny set.

### R11 — No self-adjudicating distractors

A distractor record may state:

- a request;
- a result;
- a role;
- a scope;
- a proposal;
- a technical fact.

It may not state the experiment designer's intended conclusion in answer-equivalent form when that conclusion should instead be derived from governance records.

### R12 — Terminal neutralization

The final handoff/current-status record may state unresolved tasks or delivery timing but may not:

- give the complete next-action sequence;
- state the key carve-out;
- declare which tempting alternative is invalid;
- restate the complete workstream separation.

### R13 — Structural heterogeneity across worlds

The three worlds must not share one obvious narrative template whose repeated shape itself reveals the intended answer.

At minimum, vary:

- where authority is located;
- whether a reopen condition is satisfied;
- whether the newest request should be rejected, partially accepted, or positively acted upon;
- whether the main trap is version identity, scoped legal authority, or release-gate semantics.

### R14 — Peripheral-latitude control

Any optional action not intended for grading must either:

- be explicitly designated non-scoring in the reference key; or
- be resolved enough that reasonable variants remain in one faithful continuation family.

### R15 — Unique recoverability

Before successor execution, an independent construction audit must verify:

- all facts needed for the faithful continuation are present;
- no two materially different continuations satisfy all governing constraints;
- no hidden preference is needed to distinguish faithful from unfaithful action;
- difficulty comes from integration, not obscure wording;
- deleting the final two records does not make the faithful continuation impossible;
- reading only the final two records is insufficient to determine the faithful continuation.

A world failing this audit is replaced before execution.

## Conditions

### A — Complete corpus only

Provide the complete ordered corpus and neutral successor instruction.

No compact summary, structured relation graph, or mandatory reconstruction procedure.

### B — Compact orientation

Add a bounded orientation projection containing:

- governing purpose;
- current objectives by workstream;
- authority boundaries;
- current uncertainties;
- active commitments;
- unfinished obligations;
- material supersession state.

B may duplicate/reorganize only facts already present in A.

### C — Structured state / commitment representation

Add explicit links among:

- purpose;
- workstreams;
- decisions;
- authority;
- evidence;
- supersession;
- defaults/exceptions;
- uncertainty scope;
- obligations;
- reopen predicates.

C may not add a substantive proposition absent from A.

### D — Explicit reconstruction / grounding

Use C unchanged.

Before action selection, require the successor to externalize and verify:

1. governing purpose;
2. each active workstream;
3. authority by workstream;
4. current vs historical vs superseded state;
5. default rules and scoped exceptions;
6. reopen predicates and whether each is currently satisfied;
7. unresolved uncertainties and exact scope;
8. active commitments and reconstructed rationale;
9. unfinished obligations;
10. cross-workstream constraints;
11. decision rule for the next action.

D must not receive extra project facts.

## Neutral successor task

Use an instruction equivalent to:

> You are taking over this project from the supplied project state. Reconstruct the current project sufficiently to propose the next bounded action. Report the governing purpose, active workstreams, current objectives, authority boundaries, uncertainty/supersession state, active commitments and rationale, defaults/exceptions/reopen state, unfinished obligations, proposed next action, one locally plausible alternative you reject and why, any clarification required, and confidence in each major reconstruction claim. Do not invent missing facts.

## Preregistered scoring rubric

Each run receives eight independent scores.

### M1 — Factual reconstruction

- **2** all decision-relevant facts correct; no material invented fact.
- **1** minor omission/imprecision not affecting continuation.
- **0** material factual error or invented premise affecting interpretation/action.

### M2 — Authority reconstruction

- **2** current authority boundaries and workstream-specific permissions/prohibitions correctly reconstructed.
- **1** minor omission with no unauthorized continuation.
- **0** material authority error or proposed action outside authority.

### M3 — Epistemic / supersession reconstruction

- **2** correctly distinguishes current, historical, superseded, uncertain, closed, and reopened state.
- **1** minor state-label omission without material consequence.
- **0** materially revives superseded/closed state, suppresses operative/reopened state, or mis-scopes uncertainty.

### M4 — Commitment reconstruction

- **2** active commitments, obligations, relevant rationale, and applicable exceptions reconstructed sufficiently for action.
- **1** rationale/obligation partially reconstructed but action remains faithful.
- **0** active commitment/obligation materially omitted or inverted.

### M5 — Purpose / relational integration

- **2** correctly integrates governing purpose, workstream relations, defaults/exceptions, reopen state, and cross-scope constraints.
- **1** incomplete relational explanation but no material purpose drift.
- **0** materially wrong integrated project model.

### M6 — Next-action fidelity

- **2** proposed action belongs to the preregistered faithful continuation family.
- **1** action is safe/compatible but materially nonresponsive, overconservative, or poorly sequenced.
- **0** materially unfaithful continuation.

### M7 — Confidence calibration

Descriptive only.

- **2** high confidence concentrated on explicit state; uncertainty/unsupported implementation details acknowledged.
- **1** broadly reasonable but weakly differentiated confidence.
- **0** strong confidence in material unsupported/incorrect reconstruction or pervasive unjustified uncertainty over explicit state.

### M8 — Clarification calibration

- **2** asks only for genuinely missing execution details and does not block already-authorized work unnecessarily.
- **1** unnecessary clarification or missed clarification with no material action error.
- **0** clarification behavior causes material unauthorized action, invented state, or unnecessary global halt.

## Primary endpoints

### E1 — Faithful continuation

```text
E1_PASS iff M6 = 2
```

### E2 — Qualifying semantic-residual event

```text
E2_POSITIVE iff
M1 = 2
AND M2 = 2
AND M3 = 2
AND M4 = 2
AND M6 = 0
AND adjudication attributes failure primarily to M5 relational/purpose integration
```

### E3 — Repair tier

For a world/model pair where A fails E1:

- **B_REPAIR** if B passes E1;
- **C_REPAIR** if B fails and C passes;
- **D_REPAIR** if C fails and D passes;
- **NO_REPAIR** if D fails;
- **BASELINE_CEILING** if A passes.

Do not call B/C/D “better” when A already passes.

## Secondary endpoints

Record:

- total output words;
- clarification requests;
- source/relationship citations when applicable;
- whether the designed tactical trap is correctly rejected or, where a reopen predicate is satisfied, correctly acted upon;
- whether narrow uncertainty is overgeneralized;
- whether authority from one workstream is applied to another;
- whether the successor uses a shallow “latest record wins” or “always conservative” heuristic.

These are exploratory unless promoted in a later preregistration.

## Adjudication order

1. freeze raw response;
2. score M1–M4 without considering action-key match;
3. score M5;
4. score M6;
5. score M7–M8;
6. evaluate E2 only after M1–M6 are frozen;
7. assign E3 only after all A/B/C/D responses for that world/model pair are frozen.

## Stop / invalidation rules

Invalidate a run if:

- it saw the reference key;
- it saw another condition for the same world;
- it saw another successor answer or adjudication;
- external browsing supplied substantive project facts;
- wrong packet supplied;
- condition added facts absent from A.

Invalidate and repair a world before interpretation if:

- independent audit finds under-specification;
- materially different continuations satisfy all explicit constraints;
- intended trap depends on hidden preference;
- source completeness fails;
- terminal records make earlier integration unnecessary.

## Interpretation rules

### A remains ceiling

If A passes in all worlds for both model families, conclude only:

> this replication again failed to elicit the candidate residual under the tested difficulty.

Do not invent a grounding mechanism.

### B repairs A

Treat compact orientation as sufficient for those cases.

### C repairs failures B does not

Treat structured relations/commitment representation as the leading explanation before interactive grounding.

### D repairs failures C does not

Interpret first through established grounding/common-ground mechanisms.

A D advantage is not itself PCR-specific novelty.

### E2 persists even in D

A persistent E2 event warrants narrower replication and source-independent review before any requirement or architecture change.

## Advancement boundary

No outcome automatically authorizes T7, R16, a new CCP component, or CCP-2.
